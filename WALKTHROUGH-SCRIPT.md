**0:00–0:20 · Introduction and role**
“Hi, I’m Nkwa Onwudiwe. For SI 511 Product Discovery, I worked with Creation Crew on a concept for Michigan Recreation. My contributions included opportunity research and outcomes, service blueprinting, technical feasibility, and secondary research.”

**0:20–0:48 · Problem and intended value**
“Many students have short windows to work out. Crowded spaces and unavailable equipment make it difficult to use that time effectively. Michigan Recreation is the institutional customer; students are the end users. Our goals were to help students complete workouts more efficiently and give staff useful information for facility operations.”

**0:48–1:18 · What the testing changed**
“We tested four assumptions with a small exploratory sample of fourteen students. Thirteen reported equipment-related issues, but only two actually checked gym crowd levels before going. Ten said they adjusted their workouts because of conditions. That suggested we should focus on actionable equipment availability and workout alternatives, not just another crowd indicator.”

**1:18–2:18 · Show three moments in Figma Make**

“First, here is how a student can check facility demand. Next, I’ll look for equipment information. And when the intended option is unavailable, this concept shows a way to explore an alternative so the workout can continue. These are simulated prototype states, not real-time facility data.”

**2:18–2:45 · Limitations, feasibility and reflection**
“My feasibility contribution also looked at the systems behind the experience: check-in data, possible zone sensing, and the harder challenge of equipment-level monitoring. We proposed starting with facility-level information and testing the more complex elements carefully. The prototype does not prove that waiting time improves, so my next step would be task-based usability testing and a small pilot with Michigan Recreation.”

**2:45–2:55 · Conclusion**
“The main lesson I took away is that a real pain point does not guarantee users will adopt the first proposed solution. Useful, trustworthy, actionable information matters.”
